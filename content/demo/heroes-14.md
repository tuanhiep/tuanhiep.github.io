Tuấn by the wrist and bound him with a bitter oath.</span> <span class="va-sent" role="button" tabindex="0" data-va-cue="e2" data-va-track="en">Years earlier, state politics had forced Liễu’s family aside in favor of his brother Emperor Thái Tông’s branch; on his deathbed, Liễu commanded his son to seize the throne someday to avenge his father’s wounded pride.</span></p><p class="va-para"><span class="va-sent" role="button" tabindex="0" data-va-cue="e3" data-va-track="en">Quốc Tuấn bowed silently—yet carried that agonizing weight in his heart for thirty years without ever drawing a blade against his kin.</span> <span class="va-sent" role="button" tabindex="0" data-va-cue="e4" data-va-track="en">When Kublai Khan’s Mongol empire swallowed China and pointed its spears toward Đại Việt, Chancellor Trần Quang Khải—a son of Emperor Thái Tông and now the chief pillar of court—harbored quiet dread of his cousin Quốc Tuấn’s rising military command.</span> <span class="va-sent" role="button" tabindex="0" data-va-cue="e6" data-va-track="en">Knowing Quang Khải famously disliked bathing, Quốc Tuấn waited for a quiet river encampment and approached his rival with a basin of warm, fragrant herbal water and a gentle smile.</span></p><p class="va-para"><span class="va-sent" role="button" tabindex="0" data-va-cue="e7" data-va-track="en">“Cousin, your robes are dusty from class="va-sent" role="button" tabindex="0" data-va-cue="e9" data-va-track="en">Stunned, Quang Khải unfastened his tunic while the Supreme Commander of Đại Việt scrubbed his shoulders clean.</span> <span class="va-sent" role="button" tabindex="0" data-va-cue="e10" data-va-track="en">Smiling, Quốc Tuấn remarked, “Today the Duke of Quốc Công gets to bathe the Grand Chancellor!”</span> <span class="va-sent" role="button" tabindex="0" data-va-cue="e11" data-va-track="en">Laughing until tension melted like river mist, Quang Khải replied, “Today the Grand Chancellor is bathed by the Duke of dragon columns as the sovereign asked a single question:</span></p><p class="va-para"><span class="va-sent" role="button" tabindex="0" data-va-cue="e21" data-va-track="en">“The Northern armies are vast and fierce.</span> <span class="va-sent" role="button" tabindex="0" data-va-cue="e22" data-va-track="en">Should we make peace, or should we fight?”</span></p><p class="va-para"><span class="va-sent" role="button" tabindex="0" data-va-cue="e23" data-va-track="en">Like ten thousand bamboo groves snapping upright in a single gale, the elders raised gnarled hands and roared in one thunderous voice:</span></p><p class="va-para"><span class="va-sent" role="button" tabindex="0" data-va-cue="e24" data-va-track="en">“Đánh!” (Fight!)</span></p><p class="va-para"><span class="va-sent" by mastering time, space, and terrain.</span> <span class="va-sent" role="button" tabindex="0" data-va-cue="e26" data-va-track="en">As Toghan’s host flooded southward, Quốc Tuấn executed a role="button" tabindex="0" data-va-cue="e27" data-va-track="en">Deprived of forage and scorched by relentless tropical heat, the overextended Mongol army grew sluggish and hollow.</span> <span class="va-sent" role="button" tabindex="0" data-va-cue="e28" data-va-track="en">Within months, Quốc Tuấn’s counteroffensives at Hàm Tử, Chương Dương, and Tây Kết shattered Toghan’s hold and drove the northern host back across the border.</span></p><p class="va-para"><span class="va-sent" role="button" tabindex="0" data-va-cue="e29" data-va-track="en">When Toghan and Admiral Omar returned in 1288 data-va-cue="e33" data-va-track="en">Twelve years later, in 1300, as the seventy-year-old commander lay dying at his Vạn Kiếp estate, Emperor Anh Tông hurried to his bedside and asked anxiously what future generations should do if northern legions ever returned.</span></p><p class="va-para"><span class="va-sent" role="button" tabindex="0" data-va-cue="e34" data-va-track="en">Speaking calmly of history from Triệu Vũ Vương to Lý Thường Kiệt, the sage left one supreme rule above every tactical manual: keep commanders and soldiers united like father and son, pit agile short strikes against
